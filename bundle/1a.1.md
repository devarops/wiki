title: Elements of Style for Paragraphs
description: Rules making the paragraph the unit of composition, with sentence and document guidance.

The paragraph is the unit of composition: one paragraph to each topic.
The goal of this structure is to aid the reader.
Begin each paragraph with a topic sentence stating its purpose.
Succeeding sentences explain, establish, or develop the topic sentence.
End the paragraph by emphasizing the topic sentence or stating a consequence.
Avoid ending with a digression or unimportant detail.
The same principle scales up to whole documents: each section acts as a paragraph with its own topic sentence.
Reports often follow outline structures, such as setting, plot, characters, and purpose for a novel.
At the sentence level, keep related words together so their relationship stays clear.
Place the emphatic words of a sentence at the end.
Express co-ordinate ideas in similar form, following the principle of parallel construction.
In summaries, keep to one tense to avoid appearing uncertain.
Like the one-idea-per-card discipline of [Zettelkasten](./1a.md), one-topic-per-paragraph keeps each unit focused.
